Eikon · design review · 31 October 2026

Fifty-three good ideas, and not one great screen.

A pharmacist opens Eikon for one of maybe five reasons: log the fridge, sign the register, hand a customer their order, close the till, look something up. Today the app greets them with a 4.9-second animation, a choice between three portals (two of them greyed out), a rail of 50 items in seven uppercase categories, and a first screen with 77 buttons before a single record exists.

Master Universal just proved the other way works. One question per screen, one button per row, the search box as the biggest thing on the page. Eikon has more functionality than Master Universal will ever have. That is exactly why it needs the same discipline more, not less.

Nothing in the repository was changed. The "Today" screens are real renders of the current ui/ build served locally against an empty demo pharmacy. The "Reimagined" screens are HTML mockups at the same 1110×680 window size used for the Master Universal review. Every current module keeps a home; the map at the end shows where.

THE DIAGNOSIS

The interface is a map of the codebase

Eikon is built as 53 modules, each a file, each a sidebar entry, each with its own toolbar, its own date picker, its own Save, its own Print and its own idea of what a button looks like. The sidebar is literally the list of files: Inspections, Operations, Sales & Clients, Inventory, Communication, Clinical, Reference. Those are an engineer's folders. No pharmacist has ever thought "I need to go to Operations".

The first screen tries to help by summarising everything, so it becomes everything: 18 rows, each with a status pill, an "Open" and sometimes a "Quick enter", loading from 35 requests with its own Refresh button. A screen that needs a Refresh button is telling you it does not trust itself.

And because every module is equally important, the app ships a 1,953-line manual with 39 chapters, reachable from a "Help & Guides" button pinned to the rail. If the interface needs a manual, the interface is the problem.

50 → 4places in the rail. Today, Customers, Stock, Registers. Team and Settings sit at the bottom where set-up belongs.
77 → 9buttons on the first screen, and each of the nine says what it does: "Log", "Confirm", "Collected".

THE FOUR MANDATES, APPLIED

What simplicity costs, line by line

Ruthless subtraction

Every control was asked one question: does the pharmacist need this to finish the thing they came to do? The intro, the portal picker, the module search, the accordions, Refresh, Load, "Sync queued", "Cloud: ON", "dbg=0" in the corner of Temperature, Fullscreen, Diag, the Help manual. All gone. Not hidden in a menu. Gone.

The 3-action rule

Logging the fridge today is menu, module, min, max, Save: five actions, after a five-second film. Reimagined: the fridge row is already on Today with two live fields. Type, Tab, type, Enter. The table below holds every core task to three or fewer.

Zero cognitive load

The rail uses words a grandparent would use: Customers, Stock, Registers. Status is a sentence, not a code: "Last cleaned 14 days ago" instead of a red pill that says Overdue next to a button that says Open. Dates are words: "Due Tuesday". Nothing uppercase shouts at you.

Flawless detail and flow

One typeface, one light surface (a pharmacy is a bright room; a dark screen among white shelves looks like a developer tool), one accent used with intent, one icon set instead of emoji. Registers save on Enter and show "Saved 09:12 · Maria" with a ten-second Undo. No modal for primary work. No Save button for a single number.

1 · THE FIRST SCREEN

Open on what needs doing, in the order it needs doing

The reimagined home is Today. It is the Dashboard, the quick-entry modals, the Instructions handover and the alerts, merged into one list that reads top to bottom like the morning itself. A headline says how many things need you. The things are listed in the order they need you: the registers that must be filled before the doors open, then the customers who are waiting. Each row has one button, and the button says what it does.

The fridge row does not open anything. Its two fields are right there. You type 3.9, Tab, 6.1, Enter, and the row turns green and says who logged it. That is the whole temperature module, for the 360 days a year when nothing is wrong.

The search box is the biggest thing on the screen because it replaces the entire rail for anything not on Today. Type a customer's name and Enter opens their page. Type "OCP" and the finder appears. Type "Enalapril" and you see stock, the Cherubino order and the near-expiry line in one answer. Search is how 40 of the 53 modules are reached from now on. They did not deserve a rail slot. They deserve to be one keystroke away.

Where today's features went

  • Dashboard rows + Quick enter modals become Today rows that act inline.
  • Instructions & handover, Chat Board become the Team tile and a handover line on Today.
  • Alerts, Scarce stock, Near expiry, Supplier updates become the Stock tile, which names the item.
  • End Of Day becomes the Takings tile with one button at closing time.
  • Shifts (coverage) becomes the Team tile: who is in, until when.
  • Module search, categories, Refresh, Fullscreen, Logout buttons are removed. Your name at the bottom of the rail is the account menu.
2 · ONE CUSTOMER

A customer is one person, not five modules

Today a customer lives in Client Orders, Quotations, Order Diary, Client Deliveries, Client Tickets, Appointments and Loyalty & Campaigns, with Vaccines, POCT and Screening Campaigns in a different category altogether. Seven places for one person, and in none of them can you see the other six.

Reimagined: Customers is a narrow list on the left and one person's story on the right, exactly the shape Master Universal now uses for a patient. Name at 24px, two status pills, four facts, one primary button that changes with their state, and underneath it everything they have asked for or been given, in time order. Orders, deliveries, questions, appointments, vaccines and blood pressure readings are not destinations. They are lines in the story and chips you filter by.

When the pharmacy has a Master Universal licence, the same list grows a Patients chip and the same page shows the dispensing side: next due, bookings, what is owed. One person, one page, whichever system first met them.

Where today's features went

  • Client Orders, Quotations, Order Diary, Deliveries become the Orders chip and the "New order" button.
  • Client Tickets become Questions, with "Reply" as the row's button.
  • Appointments become a line on the person and a day view under Team.
  • Loyalty & Campaigns become one fact (points) and one line per campaign.
  • Vaccines, POCT, Screening become Services given to this person.
  • Contacts becomes the same list, filtered to doctors and suppliers.
3 · THE REGISTERS

A register should look like the book the inspector wants to see

Temperature, Cleaning, Daily Register, Locum Register, the four DDA screens, Certificates, End Of Day, Paid Out, Credit Notes, Locum Receipts and the Audit Trail are all the same thing: a legal record, kept by date, signed by a person. Today each has a different toolbar. Temperature has Load, Sync queued, Print dashboard and Save. End Of Day has Save, Report, Print, Lock, Unlock and Audit Log, plus three pills that say Unlocked, Not Saved and Cloud: ON. Nobody at the counter should ever read the words "Cloud: ON".

Reimagined: Registers is one place, one shape. Pick the register with a chip, see the month as a calendar of entries, and today's cell is already editable. Entries save when you press Enter, keep who and when, and cannot be deleted, only corrected with a note, which is what the law wants anyway. "Print the month" is one quiet link, because the inspector is the only person who prints.

The daily registers do not even need visiting on a normal day. They are filled from Today. Registers is where you go to look back, which is rare, and where the inspector goes, which is rarer.

Where today's features went

  • Temperature, Cleaning, Daily Register, Locum Register are chips. Entry happens on Today.
  • DDA Purchases, Sales, Stock Takes, POYC are one DDA register with four chips, not four modules.
  • End Of Day, Paid Out, Credit Notes, Locum Receipts are the money registers. Lock happens on Done with a ten-minute Undo.
  • Certificates are the register of documents, with "expires in 30 days" showing on Today when it matters.
  • Audit Trail stays, as a register. It stops being a button on every other screen.
  • Load, Sync queued, Print dashboard, Refresh, dbg, Cloud: ON are removed. The app is always in sync or it says plainly that it is not.

Every core task, counted

An action is a click, a tap or a field typed into. Opening the app is not counted, and neither is the current 4.9-second intro, though a pharmacist counts both. "Today" counts the shortest route in the current build.

6 · THE BRIDGE TO MASTER UNIVERSAL

One patient book, true on every PC

Three asks are really one design decision. Pharmacies want to see their Patient Manager data online, want printable patient books, and have the problem that two PCs in the same shop each keep their own copy of the patients and never agree. All three are solved by making Eikon the shared book and the Patient Manager on each PC a pen that writes in it.

The Patient Manager already speaks to Cloudflare. Every patient opened and every posting fires a detached /event ping to the worker today, for the usage counters, and the Executioner hands it the record at POST. The pipe exists. The change is what travels through it: the booking, the ready, the collected, the note, as the events they already are in inbox.dat. Eikon keeps the merged book per licence and gives it back to every PC that asks. Nothing new to install, no sync button, no setting. A ready on PC 1 shows on PC 2 before the pharmacist has walked over to it.

In the browser, the Patients chip under Customers shows the same Today and Patient screens the Master Universal review designed, pixel for pixel. A pharmacist who learned one has learned the other. "Print the book" is one link on the patient: visits, bookings and the DDA register for that person, from the same data.

The rules, so it stays simple

  • Offline first. Each PC keeps working from its own files when the internet is down, exactly as now. It catches up when it returns.
  • Ten seconds. A change on one PC appears on the others within ten seconds. If it cannot, the rail says "Working alone since 09:12" in plain words.
  • Last event wins, per field. Two PCs marking the same patient Ready is not a conflict. The event log is kept whole, because the DDA register needs it.
  • Dispensing records stay encrypted. Eikon stores what the pharmacy's records key produced. The browser unlocks it with the same password and 2FA, behind the same lock screen as the PC.
  • Names and bookings travel, nothing else. What the patient book needs, under the pharmacy's own account, and only for pharmacies that switch it on.
  • Never remove. Same rule as the licences: the cloud book only ever adds. A delete is an event that hides, not a row that vanishes.

8 · HOW TO BUILD IT WITHOUT BREAKING ANYTHING

One screen first, the way Master Universal did it

This is a structural rewrite of the front end and the riskiest change the product has had. So it is not done as a rewrite. The worker and every API stay as they are. The new shell is a second entry point behind one flag, and the old UI remains the fallback the whole way, the same arrangement the Patient Manager uses today.

Ship Today first. Just the first screen, with inline logging for the fridge, the daily register and cleaning, and the Customers waiting list reading from the existing orders and tickets endpoints. Give it to two pharmacies for two weeks and measure three things: seconds from opening Eikon to the fridge being logged, clicks per pharmacist per day, and how often the old UI gets opened. If those numbers move, port Customers, then Registers, then Stock, one at a time, each tested, each with the old screen a click away.

Everything in this review is a relayout of data the worker already serves. Nothing here needs a new table. The only genuinely new engineering is the shared patient book, and that is a worker change, not a screen.

The one sentence for the coding team. Build Today, make it the opening screen, keep the old UI one click away, and do not touch anything else until two pharmacies have lived with it for two weeks. If they love it, port the rest one place at a time. If they do not, you have lost one screen, not a product.
